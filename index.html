01 / DIPLOMA THESIS · THE PROBLEM

Integrated Power & Thermal Management for a Hybrid-Electric Regional Aircraft

Diploma thesis, 2026 · Laboratory of Thermal Engines, University of Patras · Supervisor: Assoc. Prof. T. Nikolaidis · sole author · MATLAB/Simulink

The question

Electrifying a regional turboprop pushes hundreds of kilowatts through motors, inverters, converters and batteries. Each component has its own temperature limit and mission-dependent heat load, and every watt of cooling costs pump power, mass and ram-air drag.

Which combination of cooling technologies keeps every component within its limit, over a real mission, for the least energy?

01 / DIPLOMA THESIS · METHOD

A mission-driven model, checked before it was trusted.

Electrical losses become heat loads; heat loads become coolant flow, pressure drop and pump energy at each of 705 mission time steps.

  1. Mission profileShaft power per phase · ambient temperature
  2. Power split & sizingEngine at cruise power · EPS covers the gap
  3. Loss modelsMotor · inverter · converter · bus · battery
  4. Thermal logicPassive first → PCM buffer → active loop
  5. HydraulicsMass flow · ΔP · pump power
  6. MetricIntegrated pump energy within thermal limits

Validation vs reference data

≤ 4%Motor efficiency error vs reference machine; ≈ 1.5% at high power
≤ 0.45%Inverter efficiency error vs reference model
0.17%SFC error of the gas-turbine model vs PW127 (pressure ratio 0.13%)

Design finding

Tripling battery PCM from 20 to 60 kg raised BC-loop pump energy by 17%. A thicker layer stores more latent heat but lengthens the conduction path. Contact area (fins), not mass, is the design lever. 20 kg per component was selected.

Control logic, every time step

  1. Passive cooling first
  2. PCM absorbs heat until fully melted
  3. Liquid loop runs at the smallest flow that holds the limit

The trade-off

Jet impingement cut the inverter peak by 3 °C, which let the motor–inverter loop run at under half the flow. The cost was a motor peak rising from 143 °C to 207 °C, shrinking its margin to the 220 °C limit from 77 °C to 13 °C. The hybrid works because it spends margin where margin exists.

Limitations. Abrupt steps in some temperature traces come from the PCM/active state-tracking logic, not physics. Jet impingement is correlation-based, not CFD-resolved.

Next steps. CFD-resolved jet impingement, spatial PCM melting, finned-PCM optimisation and adaptive flow control for real-time thermal management.

02 / STRUCTURAL DYNAMICS & AEROELASTICITY

Dynamic & Aeroelastic Analysis of an Aircraft Wing

Course project, Dynamics of Aircraft Structures 2025–26 · team of two · 8.9 m composite wing · FE beam model written in MATLAB

Idealised cantilever shapes and a single-mode response model (damped case: illustrative ζ = 1%); frequencies from the FE model.

Aeroelastic extension

Section idealisation20 m/s70 m/s
Closed box beamStableStable*
Open I-beamStable; much larger tip twistStatic divergence

Divergence speed scales with the square root of torsional stiffness, so the structural idealisation is part of the stability prediction. *Very stiff closed-box representation; not treated as a realism claim.

Model

  • 120 elements, chosen by convergence of the first 10 natural frequencies → 363 × 363 mass and stiffness matrices.
  • Forced response by modal superposition, stiffness-proportional damping and complex eigensolution.
  • Direct integration: explicit central difference vs implicit Newmark.

Numerical insight

The explicit scheme's step is governed by the highest mesh frequency (78 kHz): Δt ≤ 2.6 × 10⁻⁵ s, against 0.062 s for Newmark. That is roughly 2,400× more steps for the same physics.

Method

N, Q, M diagrams → modulus-weighted section properties → direct stress from pressure, inertia and thermal strain → shear flow → von Mises at critical stations → mass.

What sized it: temperature, not pressure

Across a 272 K drop the aluminium skin wants to shrink while the CFRP barely moves. The skin ends up in tension and the stringers in compression.

Free thermal strain over the 2 m length (α = 21 vs −0.5 × 10⁻⁶ /°C).
